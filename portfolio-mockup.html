The problem

Revenue reporting & analytics surface

4.6★ CSAT Top requested feature 2022–2023
Reporting interface
The problem
Merchants routinely exported raw CSVs to understand their business — a sign Stripe's own reporting wasn't meeting their needs. The existing reports were static snapshots with no filtering, grouping, or custom date logic.
What I did
Ran a focused 3-week research sprint with finance leads at 12 merchant companies, defined a reporting model flexible enough for solo founders and enterprise finance teams alike, and designed a query-builder interface that hid SQL complexity behind plain-language controls.
My contribution

Led design solo on this project. The query-builder interaction model was something I pushed for internally after seeing how Notion's filter system had changed user expectations.

Merchant design system — token refresh
Rebuilt Stripe's merchant-facing token architecture to support theming and improve cross-team consistency.
6 products adopted 2024
Token system overview
The problem
The dashboard redesign exposed how inconsistent Stripe's token layer was across merchant surfaces — different teams had diverged on color usage, spacing, and type scales. Theming (a long-requested feature) was effectively blocked without fixing the foundation.
What I did
Audited token usage across 4 merchant-facing products, proposed a 3-tier system (primitive → semantic → component), and worked with a frontend engineer to ship the new system alongside the dashboard redesign. Also wrote the adoption guide used by 6 downstream teams.
My contribution

Drove the initiative independently — this wasn't on any roadmap initially. I made the case for it by showing how it would unblock theming and cut component maintenance time by roughly half.

The problem
Engineering teams were exhausted by Jira's complexity. Linear's bet was radical simplicity — but "simple" is the hardest design target to hit without accidentally making something shallow.
Hero image — final design or key screen
How I approached it
1
Embedded research — one month with real teams
Sat inside 8 engineering teams attending standups and planning sessions before touching Figma.
2
The 80/20 framework — ruthless feature scoping
Identified the 20% of features that covered 80% of real workflows. Everything else went into a "not yet" backlog.
3
Keyboard-first design
Every action designed to be reachable from the keyboard first, mouse second — forcing a clarity of model that made mouse flows cleaner too.
My specific contribution

Designed the core issue model, cycle planning view, and command palette — the three features most cited in early user reviews.

"We shipped mobile 6 months after desktop. In hindsight we should have designed them in parallel — earlier constraints would have made both better."
