Driving UI consistency through a platform-wide design systems overhaul
My role
UI/UX Intern
Timeline

Team
Design Director, PMs, Eng
Tools
Figma, Notion
The problem
Branch's platform had accumulated 12+ button styles, inconsistent spacing, mismatched input fields, and conflicting text hierarchies. These weren't just aesthetic issues — they created confusion and friction for users. The design system had become a source of debt rather than a source of truth.
UI inconsistency examples — before audit
How I approached it
1
Heuristic evaluation — first impressions as data
Before opening Figma I used my own fresh-eyes experience of Branch's platform deliberately, documenting every moment of confusion in Notion. First impressions are a one-time resource.
2
Full UI audit — 200+ inconsistencies documented
Catalogued inconsistencies across 7 major UI areas: tooltips, error messages, date pickers, navigation, form fields, buttons, and empty states — each with location, condition, and exact fix.
3
Cross-functional alignment — 20+ person team
Stack-ranked priorities with PMs and engineers, synced daily, led prototype reviews. Major design system improvements shipped across the org within 4 weeks.
4
Component standardization — atomic design principles
Consolidated and standardized UI components in Figma, reducing duplicate components by ~82%. Set clear usage guidelines for all 7 component areas.
Audit doc in Notion
Standardized component library
My specific contribution

Sole designer on this initiative. The audit doc I created became the cross-team reference used well beyond my internship.

Monterey Bay Aquarium
2023–2024 · Capstone
Designing an artifact borrowing system for aquarium educators
My role
Project Manager
Timeline
6 months
Team
2 designers, 1 researcher
Tools
Figma, Maze, Google Suite
The problem
Educators at Monterey Bay Aquarium borrowed training artifacts through a fragmented mess of in-person asks, email chains, and spreadsheets. No central inventory, no visibility into what was on loan. We heard about lost artifacts before we even opened Figma.
Final design — artifact catalog & borrow flow
Our approach
1
Field research with real educators and admins
Conducted interviews and observations on-site. Developed two key personas and journey maps surfacing the biggest friction points.
2
Storyboards & ideation
Developed storyboards showing how a solution fit into real workflows before committing to a direction. Took inspiration from e-commerce flows.
3
Usability testing — low-fi through high-fi
Tested with actual MBA employees on-site. Key insight: educators needed biological taxonomy in their search — never would have discovered this without testing.
4
Final design — searchable catalog + admin dashboard
A checkout-inspired borrowing system where educators search a catalog, see availability, and submit in a few clicks. Admins get a dashboard to manage requests and item statuses.
My role as PM

Defined scope, timelines, and team communication across 4 teammates — and was also deeply involved in shaping user flows and facilitating critique.

Reflection

"Collaborating on an internal tool taught me the importance of clarity over flashiness — and how critical it is to validate user flows early, especially for non-technical audiences."
